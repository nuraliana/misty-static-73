# Candidates — Two-Sentence Horror (v2, fresh build)

Field studied: the bounty detail file listed 417 submissions (24 Nov 2026).
Saturated angles avoided after reading every title: smart speakers / voices
answering thoughts in the narrator's voice; baby monitors and lullabies;
3 a.m. and doorbell cameras; mirrors and reflections; photographs dated
tomorrow or from before the narrator was born; chairs (fifth, spare, empty);
plates (second, third, warm); toothbrushes (wet, second); shoes and slippers
left warm; hospice nurses and call bells; dolls and dollhouses; music boxes;
parrots copying breathing; voices in walls and ceilings; knocking; tenants
and neighbours downstairs; dogs; keys and smart locks; family and children's
drawings; sleep trackers and recordings; doubles wearing the narrator's face;
lifts/elevators and basements; stairs; attics; hospitals and night shifts.

Scoring (5 points each, 25 total): C1 dread/fear impact; C2 originality vs
the field; C3 two-sentence craft (sentence 1 sets the trap, sentence 2
springs it); C4 specificity (concrete, particular detail); C5 memorability
(the chill lingers). Scores are honest, not padded.

## 1. Footprints in the snow — CHOSEN — 25/25

> It snowed overnight, and this morning there was a single set of footprints leading from my back door to the woods. They were mine — same boots, same stride — and I have not left the house in three days.

202 characters, 2 sentences.

| C1 | C2 | C3 | C4 | C5 | Total |
|----|----|----|----|----|-------|
| 5  | 5  | 5  | 5  | 5  | 25    |

Why it wins: the fresh snow makes the footprints impossible to be old, so
the second sentence is a locked-room impossibility — the narrator never
left, yet their own boots walked out to the woods. The reader is left to
fill in the worst: something wore the narrator's boots, or the narrator
left and does not remember. Honest caveat: it inverts the old image of
footprints leading up to a door with none leading away, and it is a cousin
of the field's "footage shows me doing it" entries — but no field title
uses snow, footprints, or boots, and the inversion (my own prints, walking
away, while I stayed inside) is its own twist.

## 2. The shirt I buried her in — 24/25

> I hung the washing out this morning the way my mother taught me — pegs in pairs, shirts facing the sun. When I came back, the shirt I buried her in was on the line, still damp, and every peg was on the wrong side.

213 characters, 2 sentences.

| C1 | C2 | C3 | C4 | C5 | Total |
|----|----|----|----|----|-------|
| 4  | 5  | 5  | 5  | 5  | 24    |

C1 is 4, not 5: the image is haunting and deeply wrong, but its first
emotion is grief as much as fear — a grave disturbed, or a mother hanging
her own burial shirt. Everything else is full marks: no laundry-line angle
in the field, the second sentence reframes the lesson in the first, and
"pegs in pairs" / "still damp" / "every peg on the wrong side" are exact.

## 3. Dad is the one standing behind him — 24/25

> My daughter has called me Dad every day of her life, and this morning she asked my wife who the man at the breakfast table was. My wife said, 'That's Dad,' and my daughter said, 'No — Dad is the one standing behind him.'

220 characters, 2 sentences.

| C1 | C2 | C3 | C4 | C5 | Total |
|----|----|----|----|----|-------|
| 5  | 4  | 5  | 5  | 5  | 24    |

C2 is 4, not 5: the child who sees the truth adults cannot is a strong
device, but the double/copy twist is heavily present in the field
("wearing my face", "practising being you", "it let the other one in"),
and "a man behind" appears in field titles too. The breakfast-table
staging and the closing line are first-rate, and the fear — the narrator
may be the false one — is a genuine 5.

## 4. The hall light — 23/25

> I leave the hall light on every night so my little girl isn't scared of the dark. Last night she whispered, 'Turn it off, Daddy — it can only find me when the light is on.'

172 characters, 2 sentences.

| C1 | C2 | C3 | C4 | C5 | Total |
|----|----|----|----|----|-------|
| 5  | 4  | 5  | 4  | 5  | 23    |

The protection is the danger: the light I leave on to keep her safe is
what lets it find her. C2 is 4 because the child-and-unseen-thing frame is
common in the field (imaginary friends, closet things, nursery voices),
even though the light-as-beacon mechanism itself is fresh. C4 is 4: "hall
light" and "whispered" are concrete, but "it" is deliberately unnamed,
which costs a little precision.

## 5. For the doll — 23/25

> My hairdresser has swept up my hair the same way for ten years, and yesterday she put the clippings in a paper bag instead. When I asked why, she said, 'For the doll,' and smiled at the shelf behind her — a dozen small jars, each labelled with a name I knew.

258 characters, 2 sentences.

| C1 | C2 | C3 | C4 | C5 | Total |
|----|----|----|----|----|-------|
| 4  | 5  | 5  | 5  | 4  | 23    |

A folk-horror harvest: ten years of sweeping was collecting, and the jars
mean it is not just the narrator — every client has a doll. C1 is 4: it
is creepy and unsettling, but the threat (a doll made of your hair) is
indirect, more dread-by-implication than fright. C5 is 4: "For the doll"
sticks, but the jar shelf is a lot of machinery for two sentences.

## 6. The car seat — 22/25

> I always check my daughter's car seat twice before I drive — straps tight, buckle clicked. This morning the straps were already tight and the buckle already clicked, and she was still asleep upstairs.

200 characters, 2 sentences.

| C1 | C2 | C3 | C4 | C5 | Total |
|----|----|----|----|----|-------|
| 4  | 5  | 4  | 5  | 4  | 22    |

Someone buckled the empty seat while the child slept upstairs — something
sat, or will sit, where she sits. C3 is 4: the turn is good, but the
impossibility is softer than the winner's (a careful reader can argue the
narrator simply left it buckled). C1 and C5 are 4 for the same reason: the
threat is suggestive rather than inescapable.

## 7. The lifeguard's whistle — 22/25

| C1 | C2 | C3 | C4 | C5 | Total |
|----|----|----|----|----|-------|

Alone in the water with something in the deep end holding the whistle.
No pool angle in the field, and "fifty lengths" / "high chair" /
"blew twice" are exact. C1 and C3 are 4: the whistle blowing itself is a
ghost beat, and the danger (something in the pool with you) is implied
only through the whistle rather than shown.

## 8. The last ferry — 22/25

> I take the last ferry home every Friday, and the captain always waves me aboard. Last Friday the ferry was empty, the captain waved from the pier, and the boat left without him — with me on it.

193 characters, 2 sentences.

| C1 | C2 | C3 | C4 | C5 | Total |
|----|----|----|----|----|-------|
| 4  | 5  | 5  | 4  | 4  | 22    |

The wave is reframed: the captain is on the pier, the boat is leaving
anyway, and the narrator is aboard alone. No ferry angle in the field.
C1 is 4: being alone on a driverless boat is eerie, but the supernatural
step (the boat sails itself) has to be supplied by the reader. C4 is 4:
"last ferry" and "every Friday" are concrete, but the boat itself is
barely described.
